## 1. Latest reported quarter (Q2 2026), Q3 2026 guidance, Q3 report date

### Takeaway
Q2 2026 (reported 2026-07-30) was a broad beat. Revenue was $200.6B (+20%) against guidance of $194–199B. Operating income was $27.5B against guidance of $20–24B. AWS grew 36.7% to $42.2B with a 39.4% margin, and backlog was $496B. ## 2. The core debate: AWS operating income growth vs capex growth; capex trajectory, FCF, debt funding

### Takeaway
On a YoY basis, AWS operating income (+64% in Q2) is growing about as fast as quarterly capex (+68%). However, the absolute gap is huge: TTM AWS operating income is about $55B against about $173B of TTM gross capex. FCF is negative, and capex is now partly debt-funded. In the first half Amazon raised about $67B of debt, then about $25B more in July, plus sterling, euro, CAD and CHF deals. 2026 capex guidance rose from about $200B (February) to about $220B (July), and management attributed the increase chiefly to memory costs. Management says capacity stays short through 2027.

### Cited Findings
**Capex guidance and trajectory**
- 2026 cash capex guidance is "about $220 billion," up from about $200B in February. Jassy cited higher memory costs as the main reason. The total includes non-AWS items such as robotics, semiconductors and satellites — [Let's Data Science, 2026-07-31](https://letsdatascience.com/news/aws-revenue-growth-outruns-available-ai-capacity-b0361d90). The same is reported by the [Webull transcript](https://www.webull.com/news/15364638750483456).
- The February guidance ("approximately $200B") was about +53% on 2025's $131.8B — [Zacks via Nasdaq, 2026-03-10](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/amazon-trades-premium-p-e-time-hold-or-fold-stock) **[>6M OLD]**.
- Q2 2026 "cash capex" was $53.1B, "primarily" for AWS and generative AI (Olsavsky). The cash-flow statement shows $54.2B of gross purchases (harness F149) — [Webull transcript](https://www.webull.com/news/15364638750483456).
- Quarterly gross purchases of P&E (harness F166–F170, F145, primary): Q1'25 $25.0B; Q2'25 $32.2B; Q3'25 $35.1B; Q4'25 $39.5B; Q1'26 $44.2B; Q2'26 $54.2B.
- 2027: no number was given. Jassy said 2027 capacity is "largely reserved," 2028 demand is "striking," and Amazon is "on pace to double power capacity by end-2027 versus 2025." He added that Amazon will "still not have enough capacity to meet all the demand we have in 2026," and expects the same in 2027 — [Webull transcript](https://www.webull.com/news/15364638750483456).
- AI share of capex: "the majority" goes to AI and AWS, with no percentage given — [Webull transcript](https://www.webull.com/news/15364638750483456).
- Payback: servers take "a little under three years" to break even and have 5–6 year useful lives. Data centres have 30+ year lives — [Webull transcript](https://www.webull.com/news/15364638750483456). Some servers' useful life was cut from 6 to 5 years in January 2025, and the disclosed payback covers servers only, not buildings — [Levelheaded Investing](https://www.levelheadedinvesting.com/p/amazon-com-inc-amzn-q2-2026-results-same-strategy-opposite-reaction).
- Jassy on FCF: there will be "free cash flow headwinds" until data centres come online and are monetised. He expects revenue growth to outpace incremental capex at some point, producing "very compelling" returns. He also said Amazon has issued debt this year and is "evaluating other options" for funding — [Webull transcript](https://www.webull.com/news/15364638750483456).

**AWS operating income vs capex**
- AWS operating income by quarter: Q3'25 $11.4B (with severance) **[>6M OLD]** — [Q3 2025 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000121/amzn-20250930xex991.htm); Q4'25 $12.5B (F151); Q1'26 $14.2B — [Q1 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000012/amzn-20260331xex991.htm); Q2'26 $16.6B — [Q2 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000024/amzn-20260630xex991.htm).

That is a fifth straight quarter of acceleration — [Trefis](https://www.trefis.com/stock/amzn/articles2/617445/the-dates-that-matter-most-for-amazon-stock/2026-10-02).
- Estimate: TTM AWS operating income at Q2 2026 was about $54.7B against TTM gross capex of $173.0B, a ratio of about 0.32. In Q2, AWS operating income grew +64% YoY and gross capex +68% YoY, so they are roughly at parity. D&A rose only +31% YoY ($20.0B vs $15.2B, F22/F19), so depreciation lags capex.

**Debt issuance and leases**

- The 10-Q (Note 4) reportedly shows about $650B of total contractual commitments, about $286B of them off balance sheet — [Levelheaded Investing](https://www.levelheadedinvesting.com/p/amazon-com-inc-amzn-q2-2026-results-same-strategy-opposite-reaction). This is secondary; check the 10-Q note directly: [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000026/amzn-20260630.htm).

### Inferences
- **Harness K5 / red-team signal (Q3 capex ≤ / > $62B):** at $220B, with H1 gross purchases of about $98.4B, the implied H2 average is about $61B a quarter on a gross basis, or roughly $62B on the "cash capex" basis. If spend ramps, Q4 is above Q3, so Q3 ≤ $62B is plausible. The guidance has already been raised once, though (memory costs), so this is close to a coin flip, slightly favouring ≤ $62B.
- **Red-team signal (total debt > $150B):** $128.9B plus the July $25B USD deal is already about $154B before the sterling, EUR, CAD and CHF deals and before maturities (current debt was about $3.3B, F202). **Q3-end debt above $150B is very likely**, so this pre-mortem signal will probably trip at the Q3 report. That is mechanical given known issuance, not new information.
- Bull reading: YoY parity between AWS operating income growth and capex growth, a backlog that covers years of capacity, and stated 2026–27 capacity shortages all mean the capex is demand-pulled, not speculative.
- Bear reading: depreciation (+31% YoY) is far behind capex (+68%). A future depreciation wave and off-balance-sheet commitments will weigh on AWS margins. The first-half demand concession in the bond market, with lower orders than March, signals rising cost of funding.

### Gaps
- No AI versus non-AI capex split, and no AWS-only capex figure.
- No full-year 2026 finance-lease additions found, and no total 2026 debt raised (sterling size unknown).
- No 2027 capex guidance. Expect commentary on the Q3 or Q4 calls.

## 3. AWS competitive position vs Azure and Google Cloud; Trainium, Anthropic, Bedrock

### Takeaway
AWS is the largest hyperscaler but the slowest-growing of the three. Q2 2026 growth was AWS +36.7% ($42.2B), Azure +43%, and Google Cloud +82% ($24.8B). AWS's $496B backlog is now smaller than Google Cloud's $514B and Microsoft's $678B commercial RPO. In absolute dollars, though, AWS's YoY revenue gain ($11.3B) roughly matched Google Cloud's ($11.2B). Anthropic is both AWS's anchor AI customer (more than $100B of AWS spend over 10 years, up to 5GW of Trainium) and the source of $70B+ of pre-tax mark-to-market gains in H1 2026. That creates concentration and circularity risk.

### Cited Findings
**Competitors**
- Microsoft FY Q4 2026 (reported 2026-07-29): Azure +43% YoY; Microsoft Cloud $59.3B (+27%); commercial RPO $678B, about 45% of it tied to OpenAI. The article's RPO growth figures conflict (+43% in the text vs +84% in a table). Capacity constraints reportedly eased by Q4 — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/azure-678-billion-backlog-earnings-2026/). This is secondary; verify against Microsoft's release.
- Google Cloud Q2 2026 (reported about 2026-07-22): revenue $24.8B (+82%); operating income $8.8B (35.6% margin, vs 20.7%); **backlog $514B** (vs $460B in Q1 2026 and $106B in Q2 2025). Alphabet's 2026 capex guidance is $180–190B, "higher in 2027." Alphabet's Q2 FCF was −$5.9B. TPU system sales were recognised for the first time — [Fierce Network](https://www.fierce-network.com/cloud/google-cloud-surges-alphabets-ai-capex-problem-grows).
- AWS Q2 2026: backlog **$496B, "growing triple digits" YoY** (Jassy) — [Webull transcript](https://www.webull.com/news/15364638750483456). The weighted-average remaining term is 6.4 years — [Levelheaded Investing](https://www.levelheadedinvesting.com/p/amazon-com-inc-amzn-q2-2026-results-same-strategy-opposite-reaction). The filings name no backlog customers (same source).
- Estimate: YoY dollar revenue added in Q2 2026 was AWS about $11.3B ($42.2B − $30.9B) versus Google Cloud about $11.2B ($24.8B − $13.6B). AWS's 39.4% operating margin is above Google Cloud's 35.6%.

**Trainium and custom silicon**
- Q2 2026: the chips business (Trainium and Graviton combined) passed a $25B run rate with triple-digit growth. Anthropic and OpenAI have multi-year, multi-GW Trainium commitments, and Uber and Pinterest are adopting it. Jassy sees "a real chance" of selling Trainium outside AWS — [Webull transcript](https://www.webull.com/news/15364638750483456); [Q2 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000024/amzn-20260630xex991.htm).
- Q1 2026: the chips business (Graviton, Trainium, Nitro) passed a $20B run rate. OpenAI committed to about 2GW of Trainium ramping in 2027. Anthropic will secure up to 5GW of Trainium. Amazon "landed" 2.1M+ AI chips in the trailing 12 months, more than half of them Trainium, and plans 1M+ NVIDIA GPUs from 2026. Bedrock processed more tokens in Q1 2026 than in all prior years combined — [Q1 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000012/amzn-20260331xex991.htm).

**Bedrock**
- Q2 2026: 10+ managed models were added (including GPT-5.6, Claude Opus 5, Gemma 4 and Grok 4.3). Customers spent more in Q2 than in all prior quarters combined — [Q2 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000024/amzn-20260630xex991.htm). (The Webull transcript attributes the spend comparison to an analyst question; the release states it directly.)

**Anthropic relationship**
- Investment: $8B was staged over 2023–2025. In April 2026 (announced 04-20/21) Amazon added $5B of new equity at a $350B pre-money valuation, plus up to $20B tied to commercial milestones, for **up to $33B in total**. Anthropic will spend **more than $100B on AWS over 10 years**, with up to 5GW of compute through 2036. Nearly 1GW of Trainium2/3 is expected live by end-2026 — [Global Data Center Hub, 2026-04-27](https://www.globaldatacenterhub.com/p/amazon-commits-up-to-33b-to-anthropic).
- Project Rainier: fully operational in mid-2025 with nearly 500k Trainium2 chips. The Indiana campus covers 1,200 acres and seven buildings, with a planned build-out to 2.2GW. Anthropic plans to scale Rainier to more than 1M Trainium2 chips — [Global Data Center Hub](https://www.globaldatacenterhub.com/p/amazon-commits-up-to-33b-to-anthropic).
- Multi-cloud: Microsoft and Nvidia committed $15B to Anthropic in late 2025 against a $30B Azure compute commitment. Anthropic is also on Google Cloud — [Global Data Center Hub](https://www.globaldatacenterhub.com/p/amazon-commits-up-to-33b-to-anthropic).
- Accounting gains on the Anthropic stake: pre-tax $16.8B in Q1 2026 — [Q1 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000012/amzn-20260331xex991.htm). Other income was $53.4B in Q2 2026, "primarily" Anthropic — [Q2 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000024/amzn-20260630xex991.htm).
- Anthropic revenue contribution to AWS is **not disclosed**.

### Inferences
- AWS's moat argument has shifted from "largest and most profitable" toward "largest plus custom silicon cost advantage." On growth rate and backlog size AWS is now behind Google Cloud, and Azure has OpenAI-linked backlog.
- A large share of the $496B backlog plausibly comes from Anthropic (more than $100B over 10 years) and OpenAI, which concentrates exposure on two AI labs that also buy from rivals. The scale of the backlog jump (triple digits YoY) fits that, but it is unconfirmed.
- Circularity: Amazon invests equity in Anthropic, Anthropic commits spend to AWS, and Amazon books mark-to-market gains on the stake. Investors should exclude those gains from earnings-quality assessments.

### Gaps
- No primary Microsoft or Alphabet figures were fetched; the competitor data is secondary.
- No disclosure of Anthropic's or OpenAI's share of the AWS backlog, or of AWS AI revenue.
- I did not verify the size or terms of the OpenAI–AWS contract (beyond the 2GW Trainium commitment) or the current status of Project Rainier.

## 4. Retail margin trend, robotics and automation, regionalisation

### Takeaway
Retail margins are structurally higher than a year ago. North America was 7.9% in Q2 2026, and International rose from 2.9% in Q3 2025 to 4.1% in Q2 2026. There are early cost-to-serve warning signs, though: the shipping-cost spread inverted to −2pt in Q2, partly from fuel inflation. Robotics is expanding (the robotic-arm fleet is expected to more than double in 2026), and advertising (+26%) is a high-margin tailwind inside the retail segments.

### Cited Findings
- Segment operating margins (estimates from primary segment data):
  - North America: Q3'25 4.5% (6.9% excluding the $2.5B FTC charge) **[>6M OLD]**; Q1'26 8.0%; Q2'26 7.9%.
  - International: Q3'25 2.9% **[>6M OLD]**; Q1'26 3.5%; Q2'26 4.1%.
  - Sources: [Q3 2025 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000121/amzn-20250930xex991.htm); [Q1 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000012/amzn-20260331xex991.htm); [Q2 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000024/amzn-20260630xex991.htm).
