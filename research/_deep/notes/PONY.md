### Inferences
- **Size of the robotaxi target.** Est.: "more than 3.5× FY2025" means FY2026 robotaxi revenue above US$58.1M. With US$20.6M booked in H1, H2 needs at least US$37.5M, an average of about US$18.7M a quarter. The harness KPI K2 (Q3 robotaxi revenue ≥ US$16M) is therefore consistent with management's own target. A Q3 figure below about US$16–18M would be an early sign of missing guidance.
- **TTM revenue.** Est.: Q3'25 + Q4'25 + Q1'26 + Q2'26 ≈ US$125.0M, which fills the TTM gap the harness reported.
- **Cash burn has accelerated.** Est.: H1 2026 FCF of −US$162.8M annualises to about −US$326M, against FY2025 FCF of −US$208.8M (per the harness). Est.: US$1,390.5M ÷ US$326M ≈ 4.3 years of runway at the H1 rate, below the harness's 5.6 years.
- **Operating losses are the better gauge.** GAAP net loss has been flattered by fair-value gains in three straight reported periods (Q4'25 +US$75.5M net income, FY2025, Q2'26). Operating loss and non-GAAP figures track the business more faithfully.

## 2. Robotaxi scale, permits and unit economics

### Takeaway
The fleet was 1,975 vehicles at 2026-06-30, against a target of more than 3,500 by year-end. That target was raised from "over 3,000" in March 2026, and management says it is "on track". The company claims vehicle-level unit-economics breakeven for Gen-7 in Guangzhou (November 2025) and Shenzhen (February 2026), and describes the cost definition as "comprehensive". These are company-only, unaudited figures, the absolute Gen-7 BOM has never been disclosed, and the segment economics in the 6-K cannot reproduce them.

### Inferences
- **Reported revenue per vehicle.** Est.: Q2 robotaxi revenue of US$12.07M ÷ 1,975 vehicles at period end ÷ 91 days ≈ US$67 per vehicle per day, or about US$2,000 a month. This matches the harness's "about US$2,000 a month" figure.
- **Why reported revenue per vehicle exceeds fares.** The company's own Shenzhen fare-based figure is RMB338, about US$47–49 a day. The gap is consistent with robotaxi revenue including non-fare items: the CFO said Pony "currently recognizes upfront vehicle-delivery revenue" in its joint deployment model (Section 3). It is also consistent with the average fleet being smaller than the period-end fleet. Robotaxi "services" revenue should not be read as pure fare revenue.
- **Fare revenue alone cannot carry the company.** Est.: RMB338 a day × 365 ≈ US$17k a year per vehicle. Even 3,500 vehicles at that rate produce only about US$60M a year of fare revenue, against roughly US$250M a year of non-GAAP operating expenses (Q2 non-GAAP opex of US$63M × 4). Vehicle-level breakeven is therefore far from company-level breakeven. Profitability needs a much larger fleet, higher-margin licensing or partner-funded scale.
- **Raised target, but heavy H2 load.** The fleet target was raised from >3,000 to >3,500, which is mildly positive for guidance credibility. Delivering it means adding about 1,525 vehicles in H2 2026, roughly 250 a month.

### Gaps

- No audited robotaxi segment gross profit; segment cost of revenue is not split out.
- No Beijing permit details or current Beijing fleet size were found.
- No fleet count after 2026-08-18 was found.

## 3. Vehicle funding model, OEM partners and overseas channels (Uber, Middle East, Europe, Singapore)

### Takeaway
Management describes an "asset-light" co-investment model. Overseas partners supply fleet capital and local operations, while Pony owns the fleet in key domestic markets. Uber is now "contracted" for more than 2,000 robotaxis across five European cities, inside a pipeline of more than 4,000 overseas vehicles. Revenue share, exclusivity, duration and financing terms are undisclosed. Pony also works with Bolt, and Uber works with competitors, so there is no evidence of exclusivity.

### Inferences
- **The model shifts the risk.** Partner-funded fleets lower Pony's capex per overseas vehicle. The cost is economics shared with fleet owners and platforms, plus revenue that may be lumpy because vehicle deliveries are recognised upfront.
- **Capex is still rising.** Domestic fleets remain Pony-owned, so capex keeps climbing: Q2 capex was 3.4× Q2 2025 and PP&E grew by about US$38M in H1.
- **No moat from the platform deals.** Pony is multi-homed (Uber and Bolt), and Uber is too (Section 4). The harness view that the Uber deal is distribution access, not a switching-cost moat, is supported.
- **Dubai is a near-term test.** Commercial service was planned for H2 2026. Whether it has launched is a checkable milestone for the Q3 or Q4 report.

## 6. Financial quality (non-operating gains, NCI, receivables, related parties)

### Takeaway
GAAP net results are heavily distorted by fair-value marks on a Level 3, lock-up-restricted equity stake in a GPU company that IPO'd in December 2025. A sizeable non-controlling interest also absorbs gains. About one-third of FY2025 revenue came from one related party, Sinotrans, the minority shareholder in Pony's robotruck subsidiary. Receivables nearly doubled in H1 2026. None of this is a red flag on its own, but together the evidence argues against treating GAAP net loss or "cash and investments" at face value.
