## Q2. Credit book: size, growth, NIMAL, NPLs, provisions, cards, funding

### Takeaway
The harness's biggest blind spot was credit quality, and it can now be filled. Short-term delinquencies are near historic lows: NPL 15–90 days was 7.0% in Q2'26, down from 8.0% in Q1. NIMAL recovered to 20.7% from 17.8% in Q1, though it is below about 23% a year earlier. On the other side, allowance coverage is rising, consumer coverage jumped, write-offs are heavy, the over-90-day bucket rose, and credit-card NIMAL is negative (−2.5%) because card issuance is accelerating. **Verdict: stable-to-improving on the reported early-stage metrics, with mix and duration risk building. There is no evidence of acute deterioration as of Q2'26.**

### Inferences
- Annualized H1'26 write-offs come to about 19% of average gross loans (calculated: $1,370M × 2 ÷ average of $12.5B and $16.4B). That is consistent with a high-yield, short-duration consumer and merchant book. It means the book needs constant re-underwriting, and growth masks losses: a slowdown in originations would push loss ratios up mechanically.
- Provision growth (+85%) is outrunning portfolio growth (+75%) in Q2 YoY. Part of that is CECL-style upfront provisioning on a fast-growing card book, which is consistent with card NIMAL of −2.5%. The harness test was "delinquency and allowances worsening faster than the portfolio grows" (Slater/Anderson manual check). On allowances that test is **borderline** (coverage 25.1% → 26.7%). On delinquency it is **not triggered** (15–90 NPL 8.0% → 7.0%).
- **Redteam signal at risk.** The redteam signal fires if debt exceeds $11B, against harness F45 of $10.63B. The $1.0B notes issued in September 2026 make a Q3'26 balance above $11B likely, unless other debt was repaid. This would be a mechanical trigger. Note also that the investment-grade rating and the 130bp spread weaken Ackman's "capital-markets dependence / Valeant" concern: market access is currently cheap and deep.
- Deposits ($16.0B) are about the size of the gross loan book ($16.4B). Bank licences in Mexico and Argentina would let the company convert more of the float into stable, regulated deposits and payroll accounts (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No numeric over-90-day NPL ratio has been disclosed. Delinquency aging buckets and a stage distribution were not in the fetched 10-Q extract (the last roughly 6K characters were unread).
- There is no explicit statement in the fetched sources on upfront card provisioning under CECL/IFRS 9; the topic was not discussed on the call.
- Funding mix by country (CDBs, FIDCs, bank lines) was not found.

## Q3. Margin pressure drivers and management's stated outlook

### Takeaway
The margin compression is deliberate and has several causes:
- the Brazil free-shipping threshold cut and the rapid growth of first-party (1P) sales;
- PIX discounts and lower take rates in Brazil;
- marketing for user acquisition;
- acquiring-hardware costs in Mexico;
- credit provisioning on a book growing faster than revenue.

Management gives no margin guidance and signals continued reinvestment. That makes the harness's "margin trough" thesis a bet against stated management intent in the near term.

### Cited Findings
- **Free-shipping threshold cut:** in June 2025, Mercado Livre cut its minimum order for free shipping in Brazil to R$19 (previously R$79). It was framed as an offensive against Chinese rivals. — [Mercado & Consumo, 2025-06-06](https://mercadoeconsumo.com.br/06/06/2025/ecommerce/na-disputa-do-e-commerce-mercado-livre-zera-frete-para-pedidos-a-partir-de-r-19/); [InfoMoney](https://www.infomoney.com.br/business/mercado-livre-acirra-competicao-com-frete-gratis-a-partir-de-r-19/) *(>6 months old; it is a structural driver now lapping in YoY comparisons)*
- Q1'26 letter: "Gross margin compressed by 3ppts YoY due to the lower free shipping threshold and the rapid growth of 1P." — [8-K Ex.99.1 Q1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001099590/000109959026000014/meli-20260507xex991.htm)
- Q2'26 drivers named by management. — [8-K Ex.99.1 Q2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001099590/000109959026000021/meli-20260805xex991.htm); [Q2 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/mercadolibre-meli-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
  - "The discounts in Brazil negatively impact our profitability in the short term", from PIX buyer discounts and seller take-rate discounts.
  - "Higher shipping costs that were not fully offset by higher revenue."
  - Energy-related logistics costs, called "not significant".
  - Higher POS device costs, which management attributed to memory-chip prices, plus a one-off restocking charge for devices sold at a loss.
  - Margin reflects "deliberate decisions to reinvest operating leverage in our value proposition."
- CFO Martin de los Santos:
  - Sequential margin was flat because recovering credit profitability (normalizing Brazil consumer provisions) offset the commerce and acquiring headwinds.
  - OpEx was diluted by 2.5pp QoQ, and the company "would reinvest that benefit elsewhere."
  - "We invest in a very disciplined manner." Each initiative has growth and engagement targets.
  — [Q2 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/mercadolibre-meli-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Brazil conversion rose 1.1pp YoY, which management called "a step change" that has held for a year. Brazil items sold rose 56% and items per buyer 19%. — [Q2 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/mercadolibre-meli-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Productivity offsets:
  - AI spend was about $80M in the quarter.
  - Customer-service headcount is about 7,000, down from about 10,000 four years ago while the business tripled.
  - 2026 is likely the first year in many without engineering headcount growth.
  - "Today, human written code is the exception."
  — [Q2 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/mercadolibre-meli-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Investment plan: R$57B in Brazil through 2026, including 14 new distribution centers. — [Portal Tela, 2026-03-24](https://www.portaltela.com/noticias/economia/2026/03/24/mercado-livre-aumenta-investimento-no-brasil-em-50-frente-a-shopee-e-amazon/) *(secondary source; the "50%" in the headline is unexplained)*
- Sell-side framing: Morgan Stanley (2026-03-09, target $2,800 → $2,600): "investments may aid long-term leverage but pressure near-term margins." Wedbush (2026-02-17): "competition in Brazil and higher spending." — [MarketScreener broker research](https://uk.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/MERCADOLIBRE-INC-58469/news-broker-research/) *(>6 months old)*

### Inferences
- The free-shipping and 1P effect began in June 2025. It should start lapping in Q3'26 YoY comparisons, which helps the year-on-year gross-margin picture even if absolute margins do not recover. Credit-card cohorts break even after 12–18 months, so the 2025 cohorts should turn NIMAL-positive during 2026–27. That is the mechanical basis of the "trough" thesis.
- Against that, management explicitly recycles operating leverage into the value proposition. A recovery to the FY2023–25 operating-margin range of 11–15% (the bull case) has no support in management's own statements as of 2026-08-05.

### Gaps
- No standalone logistics cost line or shipping cost per unit is disclosed.
- There is no quantified size for the PIX-discount or take-rate impact.

## Q4. Competition: Shopee, Temu, Amazon and TikTok Shop; Nubank vs Mercado Pago; market share

### Takeaway
Mercado Livre remains the clear e-commerce share leader in Brazil (about 39–47% by end-2025 estimates, depending on whether Asian cross-border players are included). The competition is real and multi-front: Amazon's fee waivers and FBA push, Shopee in low-ticket items, and TikTok Shop and Temu. In fintech, Mercado Pago is the second-largest digital player in Brazil by customers, behind Nubank, and is growing at a similar absolute pace. Hard 2026 share data is scarce; most share figures are about 9 months old.
