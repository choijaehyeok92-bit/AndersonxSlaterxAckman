## Q1. Latest reported quarter (Q1 FY27) and Q2 FY27 guidance

### Takeaway
Q1 FY27 revenue was $479.0M (+114.7% YoY, +9.6% QoQ), just above the top of guidance. GAAP operating income fell from the prior quarter. On a non-GAAP basis it rose, so the GAAP drop came from SBC and DustPhotonics purchase accounting, not from operating weakness. At the Q1 call management raised the FY27 growth outlook from ">80%" to ">85%", which implies FY27 revenue of at least about $2.47B. The beats over the guidance top have shrunk from 12–18% to about 1%.

### Cited Findings

GAAP net income $129.4M ($0.67 diluted). Non-GAAP net income $236.3M ($1.20 diluted). Cash plus short-term investments $764.3M. — [Q1 FY27 press release, 8-K Ex.99.1, 2026-09-01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026059795/credoq12027ex-991.htm)
- GAAP operating income was $120.7M (25.2% margin) in Q1 FY27, against $155.8M (35.7%) in Q4 FY26. Non-GAAP operating income rose from $216.7M (49.6%) to $230.6M (48.2%). The reconciling items were SBC of $88.0M (vs $49.7M in Q4), acquisition and integration costs of $10.4M, and amortization of acquired intangibles of $11.6M (vs $0.4M). — [Q1 FY27 press release reconciliation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026059795/credoq12027ex-991.htm)

### Inferences
- Derived: ">85%" growth on $1,335.1M means FY27 revenue of at least about $2.47B. Q1 actual plus the Q2 midpoint comes to about $1.01B. The second half would therefore need at least about $1.46B, roughly $730M a quarter, against $530M in Q2. That implies a steep second-half step-up, and the stated driver is optical (above $600M for the year, mostly in H2).
- The pattern of beats has changed. The large upside surprises of Q2–Q3 FY26 have become beats of under 1% over the top, and margin guidance has moved up to the level actually delivered. Upside surprise as a driver of the stock is fading, and the rest of FY27 now depends on the optical ramp actually happening.
- The 2026-09-02 sell-off was linked to GAAP margin compression. Most of that compression is non-cash purchase accounting (2.8pp of amortization) plus SBC. Non-GAAP gross margin held at 68%.

### Gaps
- I could not get a full verbatim Q1 FY27 call transcript; the call details above come from secondary recaps. These items are unconfirmed in primary text: the exact ">85%" wording, the H1/H2 cadence, and the split of the >$600M optical figure.
- Product vs IP revenue mix is not broken out in the Q1 FY27 10-Q, the press release or the FY2026 10-K text I searched. The 10-K says only that Credo is "a product-focused business with a strong foundation in IP".
- Credo does not guide FCF.

## Q2. Customer concentration (flagged unverified in the debate)

### Takeaway
Concentration is now verified and it is extreme. By contracting party, two customers were 71% of Q1 FY27 revenue (43% and 28%), and one of them held 57% of receivables. By end customer, four customers were 84%, the largest 33%. Concentration has eased slightly from 88% for the top three a year earlier. All sales are on purchase orders; there are no long-term take-or-pay contracts.

### Cited Findings
- Q1 FY27, contracting parties (10-Q Note 3). Revenue: Customer A 43% (50% a year earlier), Customer B 28% (35%). Receivables at 2026-08-01: Customer A 57%, Customer B 28%; at 2026-05-02 the figures were A 53%, B 20%, C 19%. — [10-Q Q1 FY27, Note 3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026060111/crdo-20260801.htm)
- Q1 FY27, end-customer view (MD&A supplement; some end customers order through contract manufacturers): Customer D 33% (33% a year earlier), Customer C 28% (35%), Customer E 13% (20%), Customer F 10% (under 10% a year earlier). These four total 84%, against 88% for three customers in Q1 FY26. — [10-Q Q1 FY27, MD&A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026060111/crdo-20260801.htm)
- FY2026, end customers: D 33% (63% in FY2025), B 32%, E 19%, for 84% across three. FY2026 contracting parties: A 49% (67% in FY2025, 39% in FY2024), B 32%. — [10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026043303/crdo-20260502.htm). Note that the letter labels differ between the 10-K and the 10-Q tables, so they cannot be matched one-to-one across filings.
- Q3 FY26: the top three customers were 39%, 32% and 17% (88%). There were four domestic hyperscalers with large YoY growth and a fifth hyperscaler secured, and management expected 3–4 customers above 10%. — [Zacks via Nasdaq, 2026-04-08 [>6 mo]](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/credo-technology-expands-hyperscaler-reach-concern-or-strength)
- Named relationships in filings:
  - Amazon held a warrant for up to 4.1M shares at $10.74, vesting on up to $201.0M of cumulative payments. It was fully vested by 2025-05-03 and fully exercised in FY2026 (net 3.8M shares). This confirms Amazon as a customer. — [10-K FY2026, Customer Warrant note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026043303/crdo-20260502.htm)
  - The 10-K says Credo built a switch cable with Microsoft (dual-ToR) and developed ZeroFlap Optics with Oracle. — [10-K FY2026, Business](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026043303/crdo-20260502.htm)
- Secondary sources: analysts linked Credo to Amazon, Microsoft and xAI. — [Benzinga, 2025-03 [>6 mo]](https://www.benzinga.com/25/03/44149586/credo-expands-hyperscaler-partnerships-with-amazon-microsoft-xai-amid-nvidia-powered-ai-boom). A newsletter says Credo cables appear in AWS Trainium racks shown by AWS's CEO and in Meta's example racks, and that four hyperscalers were each above 10% in Q2 FY26. — [The Investing Show, 2026-01-09 [>6 mo], opinion](https://theinvestingshow.beehiiv.com/p/crdo-a77e27fdf926919e)
- Contract structure: remaining performance obligations were only $4.2M at 2026-08-01 (vs $31.9M at FY2026 year-end), so purchase orders are not counted as RPO. The 10-K says purchases are made "substantially all... on a purchase order basis". The only long-term supply agreement it cites is with BizLink for AEC manufacturing, and it says customers "are unlikely to change to another design, once adopted, until the next generation of a technology". — [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026060111/crdo-20260801.htm); [10-K risk factors](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026043303/crdo-20260502.htm)
- Geography (by shipment destination), Q1 FY27 vs Q1 FY26: US $255.4M (vs $82.8M), Hong Kong $91.2M ($74.9M), Malaysia $74.5M ($6.4M), Mainland China $1.1M ($51.8M), rest of world $56.8M ($7.2M). — [10-Q Note 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026060111/crdo-20260801.htm)

### Inferences
- The largest end customer in FY2025 (63%) was very likely Amazon, given the warrant and the Trainium ramp, but the filings never name it. Labels C/D/E/F cannot be mapped to company names with confidence.
- Concentration is diversifying: the top end customer fell from 63% (FY2025) to 33% (Q1 FY27), and a fourth customer has crossed 10%. Even so, the loss or pause of any one of the top two end customers would remove 28–33% of revenue. Purchase-order terms give no contractual floor.
- Shipments to Mainland China nearly disappeared, from $51.8M to $1.1M. This probably reflects a change in where end customers' contract manufacturers sit, since Malaysia and the US rose. I found no source that explains it.

### Gaps
- No filing names the 10% customers. I found no primary disclosure of which customer is ramping or declining beyond the percentages.
- I found no disclosure of multi-year design-win contracts or volume commitments from customers.

## Q3. Product lines and runway

### Takeaway
AECs are still the engine (more than 90% of YoY growth in Q1 FY27). FY27 depends on a new optical leg: management targets optical revenue above $600M, from ZeroFlap transceivers, DustPhotonics silicon-photonics PICs and optical DSPs, each above $100M. The optical ramp, plus OmniConnect from FY28, is what has to carry growth past the AEC cycle. The CEO's pay plan is anchored to $5B of trailing revenue by FY2031.

### Cited Findings
- Credo's portfolio: AECs; ICs (SerDes and DSP-based retimers and optical DSPs); ZeroFlap Optics; active LED cables (ALCs); OmniConnect gearbox. The CEO in Dec 2025 cited "continued growth in our core AEC and IC franchises, plus the upcoming ramps of... ZeroFlap Optics, ALCs, and OmniConnect gearbox". — [Q2 FY26 PR [>6 mo]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828025054428/credoq22026ex-991.htm). In Mar 2026 the company called ZeroFlap, ALCs and OmniConnect "three new multi-billion dollar TAM expansions". — [Q3 FY26 PR [>6 mo]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026013205/credoq32026ex-9911.htm)
- DustPhotonics (closed 2026-05-28) is a silicon-photonics PIC developer. Its PICs span 400G/800G/1.6T with a roadmap to 3.2T and are "in design for leading NPO and CPO applications". Credo cites LightCounting for a SiPho PIC market of $6B by 2030. The PIC is "a foundational component" of ZeroFlap, and bringing it in-house reduces supply dependence. — [DustPhotonics deal PR, 8-K 2026-04-13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026024892/april20268-kex991.htm)
- Earlier acquisitions: Hyperlume (microLED chip-to-chip optical interconnect), $92.0M, 2025-09-29; CoMira Solutions (link-layer, FEC and security IP), $35.1M cash, 2026-02-25. — [10-Q Note 5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026060111/crdo-20260801.htm)
- Q1 FY27 brought the first silicon-photonics PIC revenue, and growth came from AECs, optical DSPs and retimers. — [Quartr summary](https://quartr.com/events/credo-technology-group-holding-ltd-crdo-q1-2027_Fktv9DKA); [Tiger recap](https://www.itiger.com/news/1122940235)
- Recent launches: a 224G-based 1.6T ZeroFlap transceiver (2026-09-15); a 1.6T ZeroFlap Active Light Cable described as "row-scale, wide-parallel" (2026-10-12); the SRMO short-reach optics standardization effort (2026-10-12). — harness evidence S25, S21, S27 (Credo IR, listed in evidence.json).
- The CEO said OmniConnect could add "thousands of dollars of Credo content per GPU" starting in FY2028. — [24/7 Wall St, 2026-10-05](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/05/credo-has-one-number-that-could-change-the-stocks-long-term-story/?.tsrc=rss)
- The Q4 FY26 call flagged supply tightness in ZeroFlap and AECs and uncertain timing of the 800G-to-1.6T transition. — [LetsDataScience, 2026-06-06](https://letsdatascience.com/news/credo-raises-guidance-as-optical-ramp-accelerates-30d77185)
- The 10-K says Credo's SerDes/DSP reaches performance similar to competitors "at a lower cost and more highly available legacy node (n-1 advantage)". It also says products "may have long product life cycles of seven years or more". — [10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026043303/crdo-20260502.htm)
- The CEO plan anchor: the FY27 CEO PSU says the "strategic plan envisions growth of the Company's trailing four quarter revenue nearly four-fold to $5 billion by the end of fiscal 2031". — [DEF 14A, 2026-08-25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026058936/crdo-20260825.htm)
- Jefferies (initiation, 2026-04-13) estimated AEC units at 8.6M in 2026 and 12.3M in 2027 in its base case, with a bull case of about 70% Credo share and about $3B of AEC revenue by 2027 (vs $2.3B consensus). It estimated ZeroFlap could exceed $300M a year. — [Invezz, 2026-04-13 [>6 mo, borderline]](https://invezz.com/en-ae/news/2026/04/13/credo-jumps-11percent-as-jefferies-backs-copper-tech-in-ai-boom-debate/)

### Inferences
- Derived: optical revenue above $600M would be about 24% of FY27 revenue at about $2.47B. An AEC-only company becomes a mixed copper and optics vendor within one year. That shift is the variant to test.
- Derived: the $5B FY2031 plan implies a revenue CAGR of about 30% from FY26 (four-fold over five years). This is below the harness's base-case 5-year CAGR of 37.1%, so the harness base case is more aggressive than management's own plan. Harness base FY31 revenue is $6.47B.

### Gaps
- There is no disclosed split of revenue by product line (AEC vs IC vs optical) in filings.
- I found no reliable 2026 third-party AEC market-share figure. The often-cited "88%" share (Investing Show) has no source or date.
- I found no PCIe/CXL retimer share data for Credo; Astera Labs dominates PCIe retimers per general coverage, but I did not verify this.

## Q4. Competition and technology substitution risk

### Takeaway
Named competitors are Broadcom, Marvell and Astera Labs, plus cable and transceiver makers. Marvell sells competing AEC DSPs and is now backed by a $2B NVIDIA investment. Substitution risk from CPO is real but is on a 2027–2028+ timeline: NVIDIA says copper stays inside the rack through Rubin Ultra, and Feynman (2028) offers both copper and CPO NVLink. Credo's DustPhotonics purchase hedges this with NPO/CPO-capable PICs.

### Cited Findings
- 10-K: "Our principal competitors... include Broadcom Ltd., Marvell Technology, Inc. and Astera Labs, Inc., as well as various cable and optical transceiver suppliers." Credo claims to be "the only company in our industry offering a complete suite". — [10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026043303/crdo-20260502.htm)
- Marvell has launched "Alaska" AECs with partners including Amphenol, plus a "Golden Cable" ecosystem initiative. Broadcom and Marvell together hold more than 80% of optical DSP share. Siemon and Volex license Credo patents. — [The Investing Show, 2026-01-09 [>6 mo], opinion, partly unsourced](https://theinvestingshow.beehiiv.com/p/crdo-a77e27fdf926919e). A sidebar headline from 2025-10-13 mentions Marvell's second-generation 1.6T AEC DSP. — [Zacks via Finviz [>6 mo]](https://finviz.com/news/169552/crdo-deepens-ties-with-hyperscalers-key-to-next-growth-phase)
- NVIDIA roadmap (GTC, 2026-03):
  - Vera Rubin uses copper to scale the Oberon rack to NVL72 and optical to NVL576.
  - Rubin Ultra (2027) Kyber uses copper NVLink up to 144 GPUs, with a CPO NVLink switch demonstrated.
  - Feynman (2028) offers both copper and CPO.
  - JPM's Harlan Sur: CPO enthusiasm is "ahead of the readiness of the downstream supply chain."
  - CRDO rose about 3% premarket while Lumentum and Coherent fell. — [Tiger, 2026-03-17 [>6 mo]](https://www.itiger.com/news/1122847052)
- NVIDIA plans to expand scale-up domains 8x with photonics. Huang: "We're going to do both". Feynman systems (mid-to-late 2028) can use copper or CPO NVLink. NVIDIA invested about $4B in Coherent and Lumentum and $2B in Marvell. — [The Register, 2026-04-05 [>6 mo]](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/05/nvidia_optical_scale_up/)
- Jefferies: CPO is "essentially a complete non-factor" for Credo's business, and Credo is "the outright technology leader". — [Invezz, 2026-04-13](https://invezz.com/en-ae/news/2026/04/13/credo-jumps-11percent-as-jefferies-backs-copper-tech-in-ai-boom-debate/)
- After Q1 some analysts read the margin compression as a sign that competitive pricing pressure in AI networking may be rising (unnamed). Needham reiterated Buy at $275, and Roth raised its target to $300 from $200. — [AskTraders, 2026-09](https://www.asktraders.com/analysis/credo-technology-shares-plunge-20-despite-earnings-beat/)
- Astera Labs is pushing Scorpio P-series fabric switches and Aries 6 PCIe 6 retimers into more hyperscalers. Broadcom's custom XPU growth (OpenAI volume in 2027) benefits Broadcom's in-house connectivity. — [Zacks via Nasdaq, 2026-04-08 [>6 mo]](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/credo-technology-expands-hyperscaler-reach-concern-or-strength)

### Inferences
- NVIDIA's own NVLink copper spine and backplane is NVIDIA-designed, not Credo AECs. Credo's AEC exposure is mainly in scale-out and ToR links and in custom-ASIC racks such as Trainium. NVIDIA's CPO move therefore threatens Credo less directly than it threatens pluggable-optics vendors. The larger risk is hyperscalers' custom racks moving to NPO/CPO in 2028+, which DustPhotonics partly hedges.
- Non-GAAP gross margin held at 68% in Q1 FY27 with guidance of 67–69%. There is no reported evidence yet of AEC price erosion. The "pricing pressure" view is unattributed analyst opinion.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable source on Chinese AEC makers or on whether hyperscalers dual-source AECs. Two things are suggestive but not proof of exclusivity: the 10-K reliance on a single AEC manufacturer (BizLink), and Marvell/Amphenol AEC partnerships.
- I could not access SemiAnalysis, LightCounting or other paywalled share and teardown data.

## Q5. Financial quality: SBC, dilution, inventory, receivables, cash, insider selling

### Takeaway
Earnings quality is acceptable but costly to shareholders. SBC is running about $88M a quarter, and the Q2 guide implies about $104M, above the red team's $100M trigger. Dilution came from SBC, a $736M ATM raise in FY2026 and $1.25B of DustPhotonics consideration, of which $310M is contingent and mostly payable in shares. Inventory growth is mostly raw materials and WIP, not finished goods. Executives sold about $290M of stock in 2026 year to date, mostly under 10b5-1 plans, with no open-market purchases.

### Cited Findings
- SBC: $88.0M in Q1 FY27 vs $35.5M in Q1 FY26 and $49.7M in Q4 FY26. R&D SBC rose $25.8M and SG&A SBC rose $21.3M YoY "driven by... new equity awards". Q2 guide: $97.0M of SBC in opex plus 1.3pp of revenue in COGS. — [Q1 FY27 PR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026059795/credoq12027ex-991.htm); [10-Q MD&A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026060111/crdo-20260801.htm)
- Derived: implied Q2 FY27 total SBC is about $97.0M + 1.3% × $530M ≈ $104M.
- ATM: in Oct 2025 Credo set up a $750M equity distribution agreement with Goldman Sachs and completed it in FY2026: 4.8M shares, $736.3M net, $13.7M costs. — [10-K FY2026, Note 1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026043303/crdo-20260502.htm); [ATM agreement, 8-K 2025-10-07 [>6 mo]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828025044360/exhibit11-8xk.htm)
- DustPhotonics consideration of $1,251.1M:
  - Cash $769.6M
  - 760k shares worth $169.1M
